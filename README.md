## Sonic Puzzle

*Find the right room for the music, using only your ears.*

You start in an empty white room. A track is playing, but it sounds wrong: the room around it doesn't suit it. Somewhere there's a room that makes it sound just like the reference recording. Change the room's **size**, **ceiling** and **material** until what you hear matches. Get all three right and the music opens up, the lights change, and the level clears.

## Running the game

1. Install **[Godot 4.7](https://godotengine.org/download)** (the standard build, not .NET).
2. Open Godot, click **Import**, and select `project.godot` in this folder. The first import takes a minute (textures and audio).
3. Press **Play** (F5), or run `godot --path .` from this folder.

**Use headphones.** The puzzle is solved by ear, and the reflections are panned left and right.

## How to play

1. **Listen to the room.** The music plays from the speakers in front of you, shaped by the room you're in.
2. **Hear the reference.** Click **Hear the reference** (top-right) to hear 8 seconds of how the track *should* sound. The room's sound drops out while it plays so you can compare. You only get **3 listens per level**, so make them count.
3. **Shape the room.** Use the panel at the bottom:
   - **Size**: Small, Medium, Large. Bigger rooms mean longer reverb and later echoes.
   - **Ceiling**: Flat, Sloped, Vault. Taller, curved ceilings stretch the tail.
   - **Material**: Plaster, Wood, Soundpanel, Concrete. This is the biggest change: foam panels soak up sound, wood is warm, concrete rings for seconds.
4. **Follow the chime.** When you pick the right option for a row, it turns green, says *Sounds right* and chimes. (A row only gives feedback once you've changed it.)
5. **Clear the level.** When all three rows are right, the level clears by itself: the dry mix blooms into the full track and the room lights up. Walk around and listen, then click **Next room**.

Clear all three levels (**Trap → Elevator Jazz → Slap House**) to finish.

### Tips

- Listen to the **tail**: how long the sound hangs after a hit tells you size and material.
- Listen to the **low end**: wood soaks up bass, while concrete and plaster let it boom.
- Walk around: you're hearing real speaker positions and wall reflections, so things change near a wall.
- Curious what's going on? **Show sound rays** (bottom-left) draws the sound bouncing around the room and shows the numbers (reverb time, echoes). It's a good way to learn the materials, but it's more fun to solve by ear first.

## For developers

All tuning (targets, stems, limits, colours) lives in `scripts/levels.gd`.

### Acoustics
Each stem plays from its own speaker in the room (`scripts/speakers.gd`, AudioStreamPlayer3D on a per-speaker bus → `Stems` bus). The reference bypasses the room on the `Reference` bus; UI sounds play on a dry `UI` bus.

`scripts/acoustics/raytraced_acoustics.gd` models the room by ray tracing against the exact room shell (sloped/vaulted ceilings included):
- Rays leave every speaker, bounce with per-band material absorption (low/mid/high), air absorption and scattering.
- Each bounce "rains" energy onto the listener, giving early reflections (→ two panned delay taps) and total reflected energy (→ reverb level).
- Traced mean free path + measured loss per bounce give RT60 per band (→ reverb length, damping, low cut). The traced mean free path matches the theoretical 4V/S for every room.
- Re-traces when the room changes or you walk somewhere new. All effect changes glide; new delay times crossfade to avoid clicks.
- **Show sound rays** (bottom-left) draws the rays and shows the numbers.

Tuning lives at the top of that file (absorption table, `WET_SCALE`, `MAX_RT60`).

### SATIE
The game only talks to `AcousticsBackend` (`scripts/acoustics/acoustics_backend.gd`): listener, speaker positions, room state + geometry (volume, area, exact shell on physics layer 2), level start/clear. To switch to SATIE, write a backend that extends it and put it on the `Acoustics` node in `scenes/main.tscn`.

### Sound effects
UI sounds are generated by `tools/make_sfx.py` (needs numpy); edit and re-run it to change them. Font: Manrope (OFL, `assets/fonts/OFL.txt`).
